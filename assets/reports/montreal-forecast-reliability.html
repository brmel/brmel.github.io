Exactly how a forecast is scored
  • Three services publish an hourly forecast a week ahead for Parc Henri-Julien and Montréal — Trudeau.
  • We read all three every five minutes and save a copy only when one of them has changed its mind.
  • An Environment Canada station beside each spot measures the real weather every minute: Parc Henri-Julien: graded from McTavish, 7.27 km away; Montréal — Trudeau: graded at its own coordinate.
  • The truth for a moment is what the station read at that moment. Not the hourly average — the reading itself.
  • Only forecasts published before that moment are scored. A value the service restated afterwards is hindsight, not a forecast, and is never counted.
  • The “how far ahead” control above the measures sets the range. At +6 h, a forecast is scored on what was on record six hours before the hour it describes.
  • Rainfall is compared against the hour it fell in, since that is the hour the station reports it for.
  • Sunshine stands in for cloud cover, which no station here measures.
  • If no reading exists within five minutes of the moment, the forecast is left ungraded rather than scored against the wrong time.
