findings:
  - "The busiest hour of the week is a tie, Tuesday and Wednesday at 2 p.m. in head-office time. Of 2,585 posts, 54.1% went out on weekdays from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., 39.5% on weekdays outside those hours, and 6.4% on weekends."
  - "Pages posting under 3 times a week earn about 5× more engagement per follower than pages posting 12 times or more (0.14 against 0.03), and the peak sits at 2 to 3 posts a week (0.42)."
  - "20% of posts went out at exactly :00, against 2% by chance, and 7 companies published 51% of them."
  - "Several images earn about double the engagement of video (0.11 against 0.05 per 1,000 followers). Inside one company the gap is smaller."
  - "About 11% of posts are labelled as promoting a product, from labels that are rough."
methods: |-
  From 6 Oct 2026 to 4 Nov 2026, I read the Posts tab of the main LinkedIn page of 99 Big Tech companies across 15 time zones, newest first. I picked the list, so it’s not a random sample. Each post’s time comes from its 64-bit URN ID, which stores creation time in milliseconds (urn >> 22), read in the company’s head-office time zone. In the 575 recent posts where I could compare it with the age LinkedIn shows, 41 went live at least 2 hours after that time, probably because they were scheduled ahead.

  Reposts are counted once each and left out of the timing analysis. Impressions are visible only to page admins, so engagement (reactions, comments and reposts) per 1,000 followers stands in for reach, measured on posts at least 72 hours old. Comparisons between groups of companies show what goes together, not what causes what.

  The list had 100 companies. I dropped Sea because the page I read was the wrong one. The feeds of AMD, Dell Technologies, Qualcomm and HP stopped loading before the window began, so their weekly rates use the days read. An AI agent labelled each post’s topic and whether it sells a product. In a spot check of 45 posts, many labels were wrong, so those charts are rough.
